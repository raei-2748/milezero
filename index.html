Every business mind starts at zero.

Mile Zero runs the Sydney Schools Business Competition, a free business case competition for students from any NSW secondary school. No finance background needed: just clear thinking, good evidence and the nerve to defend a decision.

How it works

Five weeks, four miles, one final. Each mile releases new information about the same company and asks your team for a new decision.

  1. 0

    1–2 Feb

    All teams

    Mile 0: Briefing Check

    A short check that your team has read the Opening Case. Five or six auto-marked questions, ten minutes once opened. Not scored: it just makes sure nobody starts behind.

    • One set of answers per team
  2. 1

    3–10 Feb

    All teams

    Mile 1: Where to Play

    Market data, customer interviews and a survey. Decide which markets to enter first, which to leave for later, and which to walk away from.

    • Decision worksheet
    • 150–250 word memo
    • One evidence calculation
  3. 2

    11–17 Feb

    All teams

    Mile 2: Making It Pay

    Choose how the company makes money, build a three-year plan inside the budget, and test what happens if sales come in slower than hoped.

    • Three-year and investment plan
    • 150–250 word memo
    • 2–3 minute recorded pitch (every member speaks)
  4. Schools

    21 Feb

    Schools

    School nominations

    Each school nominates one team to continue, based on Mile 1 and Mile 2.

  5. 3

    22–28 Feb

    Nominated teams

    Mile 3: Project Kestrel

    Something unexpected lands on the CEO's desk. Decide whether to accept, reject or negotiate, and show what changes in your plan.

    • Response memo (up to 400 words)
    • “What changes” table
    • One-page term sheet
  6. Final

    4 Mar

    Top 10 teams

    Live Final, Sydney Grammar School

    Present your strategy in up to seven minutes, take five minutes of judges' questions, then respond live to new information you haven't seen before.

The full route, deliverables and judging

The 2027 case

It's December 2026. A Sydney company is sitting on eight million unsold tags, and they lose value every month. The board wants a plan. Your team writes it.

“We didn't acquire eight million microchips. We acquired eight million decisions about where this company goes next.” Amelia Chen, CEO of Southern Cross Technologies, a fictional company.

Why enter

Real decisions, not trivia

You advise a company with a genuine problem, and every mile asks you to commit to a choice.

Built for beginners

Every business term is explained the first time it appears. Reasoning wins, not jargon.

Judged on your thinking

Submissions are coded so markers never see your school's name. Same case, same rubric, same start line.
