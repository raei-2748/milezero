The 2027 case

Eight million unsold tags. Losing value every month.

It's December 2026. A Sydney company has a warehouse full of stock nobody ordered, and a board with A$12 million and one question: what now? Sell cheap, build a brand, or turn a problem into a real business? Your team decides.

The route

Five weeks. Four miles. One final.

New information arrives in stages. Each stage, each mile, asks for a new decision and a new deliverable.

  1. MILE0

    Briefing Check

    A ten-minute check that you've read the Opening Case. Not scored. It just makes sure nobody starts behind.

    1–2 Feb
  2. MILE1

    Where to Play

    Market data, customer interviews and a survey. Decide which markets to enter, and which to walk away from.

    Submit: decision worksheet · 150–250 word memo · one evidence calculation

    3–10 Feb
  3. MILE2

    Making It Pay

    Three business models, a divided board and a A$12 million budget. Build a three-year plan and stress-test it.

    Submit: three-year plan · 150–250 word memo · 2–3 minute pitch, every member speaks

    11–17 Feb
  4. GATE✓

    School nominations

    Each school puts forward one team to continue.

    21 Feb
  5. MILE3

    Project Kestrel

    A surprise offer lands on the CEO's desk with a 48-hour deadline. Accept, reject or negotiate.

    Submit: response memo up to 400 words · plan changes · one-page term sheet

    22–28 Feb
  6. FINAL★

    Live Final at UNSW

    The top ten teams present for up to seven minutes, take five minutes of judges' questions, then respond live to news they haven't seen.

    4 Mar
Who can enter

Any school. Any background.

  • Students at any NSW secondary school
  • Teams of three to four from the same school
  • Up to three teams per school
  • A teacher at your school who knows you're taking part
  • Free. No paid software or databases needed.
How you're judged

Five criteria, every stage.

Problem framingDid you find the real problem?
Evidence and analysisDid you read the evidence correctly and use it?
Feasibility and numbersCould the company do this, and do the numbers add up?
Recommendation and trade-offsDid you make a clear call and admit what it costs?
CommunicationIs it clear, concise and persuasive?

Use AI for research and editing, then declare it. The analysis must be yours, and any member may be asked to explain any number. Sponsors have no say in results.

Why "Mile Zero"?

Where everything is measured from.

In Macquarie Place, a short walk from Circular Quay, stands an obelisk from 1818. Road distances in the colony were measured from it. It is Sydney's mile zero: the point everything else is counted from.

We think financial confidence works the same way. Where you start shapes everything after it. So we start everyone in the same place.

Why the ibis?

Sydney calls it the bin chicken. We call it underestimated. It adapted when its wetlands dried up, learned to eat cane toads safely and passed the trick on, and never flies alone. Egyptians made it the symbol of knowledge. Judge it by its work, not its nickname.

Questions

Before you sign up.

Do we need to know finance?

No. Terms are explained the first time they appear, and the case gives you every number you need.

How much time does it take?

A few hours per mile, spread across a week. Mile 0 takes ten minutes.

Can Year 9 and 10 students enter?

Yes. Any secondary student in NSW can enter.

Our school doesn't have a business society. Can we still enter?

Yes. You only need a teacher who knows you're taking part.

Can we use AI tools?

For research and editing, yes, as long as you declare it. The main analysis and decisions must be your team's own.
