The 2027 case

8,000,000

Eight million unsold tags, losing value every month. It's December 2026, a Sydney company is sitting on all of them, and the board wants a plan. Your team writes it.

“We didn't acquire eight million microchips. We acquired eight million decisions about where this company goes next.”

Pick a price.

You're running a drinks stall at the school fete. You can make 200 cups. Each costs $1.50, and you make them all before the gates open. Leftovers can't be sold tomorrow. You get one price.

Pick a price to see what happens. Try them all.

The $300 for cups was spent before the fete opened, whatever price you pick. That's a sunk cost: it can't change, so it shouldn't change your decision. The best price is the one that brings in the most money from here, which is $4.

Now a rival stall opens next door selling a similar drink for $4. Do you match them, or drop to $3? There's no single right answer. A good answer makes a call, uses the numbers, and admits what could go wrong. That's exactly what SSBC asks, with a real-feeling company and much bigger numbers.

Why enter

Real decisions, not trivia

You advise a company with a genuine problem, and every mile asks you to commit to a choice.

Built for beginners

Every business term is explained the first time it appears. Reasoning wins, not jargon.

Judged on your thinking

Submissions are coded so markers never see your school's name. Same case, same rubric, same start line.
